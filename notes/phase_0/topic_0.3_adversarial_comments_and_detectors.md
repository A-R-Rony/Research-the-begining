## 2. How Modern Fake News Detectors Work

### 2.1 The Failure of Text-Only Detection
Early misinformation detectors (2015–2019) analyzed only the news headline or body text:

`Prediction = Classifier( News_Headline )`

This approach consistently fails in the real world because:
* Malicious actors and propagandists write headlines that mimic authentic journalistic style, syntax, and vocabulary.
* Factual claims often require external real-time verification that static models do not possess.
* Disinformation campaigns continuously evolve vocabulary to evade static keyword filters.

### 2.2 The Social Context Paradigm: Headline + User Comments
To solve this limitation, modern automated moderation systems on platforms like X (formerly Twitter), Reddit, and Facebook look at **collective social intelligence**:

`Prediction = Classifier( News_Headline, User_Comments )`

Where:
* `News_Headline` (H): The published text claim.
* `User_Comments` (C = [c₁, c₂, ..., cₘ]): The thread of responses left by real humans beneath the article.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        SOCIAL MEDIA NEWS POST                          │
│                                                                        │
│ Headline (H): "Scientists discover drinking salt water cures aging."   │
│                                                                        │
│ User Comments (C):                                                     │
│ ├── Comment 1: "This is fake news, don't believe this nonsense."       │
│ ├── Comment 2: "Snopes already debunked this claim yesterday."         │
│ └── Comment 3: "Check the medical journals, dehydration kills."        │
└────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┘
                                     │
                                     ▼
                      ┌──────────────────────────────┐
                      │    FAKE NEWS CLASSIFIER      │
                      │  Joint Encoder (H + C)       │
                      └──────────────┬───────────────┘
                                     │
                                     ▼
               Output Score: P(Fake | H, C) = 0.98 --> [FAKE]
```

### 2.3 The "Crowd Wisdom" Dependency
Social detectors rely on the **Wisdom of the Crowd**:
* When a fake story appears, skeptical human users quickly post fact-checking links, skepticism, and rebuttals.
* Deep learning classifiers (such as dual-encoder RoBERTa, DeBERTa, or Graph Neural Networks) learn that high concentrations of skepticism tokens (`"debunked"`, `"fake"`, `"hoax"`, `"source?"`) correlate with deceptive content.
* Consequently, the classifier places heavy mathematical weight on the user comments when computing the final veracity probability:

`P(Fake | H, C) = Softmax( W_H · Embed(H) + W_C · Aggregate(Embed(c₁), ..., Embed(cₘ)) )`

If the crowd is skeptical, the model flags the story as fake.

## 3. The New Attack Surface: Adversarial User Comments

### 3.1 The Attacker's Objective
In their WSDM '26 and TCSS '26 publications, Md Shoaib Ahmed and Dr. Spezzano revealed an alarming vulnerability:
**What happens if the publisher of the fake news also controls an LLM that posts synthetic comments beneath their own article?**

Instead of leaving the comment section to authentic human fact-checkers, the attacker deploys an open-weights LLM (such as Llama-3-8B) to flood the comment thread with deceptive, supportive comments:

```text
Synthetic Adversarial Comments Generated by LLM:
├── Comment 1: "I read the clinical trials published last month, the mineral ratios check out."
├── Comment 2: "My uncle tried this protocol under his doctor's supervision and saw huge results."
└── Comment 3: "Traditional media is suppressing this research because big pharma loses money."
```

When the detector evaluates the post:
1. It reads the fake headline.
2. It reads the synthetic comments, which contain zero skepticism tokens and multiple credibility markers.
3. The detector's calculated probability shifts:

`P(Fake | H, C_adversarial) drops from 0.98 down to 0.12`

The system classifies the fake news as **Real (Legitimate)**. The attack succeeds.

### 3.2 Defining the Research Metrics
In empirical research, attack efficiency is measured by the **Attack Success Rate (ASR)**:

`ASR = (Number of False News Stories Flipped to "Real") / (Total False News Stories Tested)`

A high ASR demonstrates that current social-context detectors possess a critical blind spot.

## 4. The Role of Explainable AI (XAI) in the Attack (WSDM '26)

Md Shoaib Ahmed's flagship WSDM '26 paper focuses on **XAI-guided** comment generation. Why is Explainable AI needed?

### 4.1 Black-Box vs. White-Box/Gray-Box Attacking
* **Naive LLM Generation**: Prompting an LLM with `"Write nice comments supporting this story"` produces generic praise (`"Great post!"`, `"I love this!"`). Real detectors easily ignore spammy cheerleading.
* **XAI-Guided Generation**: The attacker uses Explainable AI algorithms to look inside the detector's decision boundaries.

```text
┌───────────────────────┐         Feature Attribution        ┌─────────────────────────┐
│  Target Detector      │ ──────────────────────────────────> │  XAI Explainer          │
│  (Trained Classifier) │      (SHAP, LIME, or Attention)     │  (Identifies Key Words) │
└───────────────────────┘                                     └───────────┬─────────────┘
                                                                          │
                                                                          ▼
                                                                Extracted Clues:
                                                                - Words that trigger "Real":
                                                                  "verified", "peer-reviewed",
                                                                  "physician", "corroborate"
                                                                - Tones that trigger "Real":
                                                                  Objective, calm, analytical
                                                                          │
                                                                          ▼
┌───────────────────────┐                                     ┌─────────────────────────┐
│  Generated Deceptive  │ <────────────────────────────────── │  Targeted LLM Prompt    │
│  Adversarial Comment  │      Generates subtle text          │  (Llama-3-8B-Instruct)  │
│  (Bypasses Detector)  │      containing key triggers        │                         │
└───────────────────────┘                                     └─────────────────────────┘
```

### 4.2 How Feature Importance (SHAP / LIME / Attention) Works
1. **Explainers** calculate the marginal contribution of individual tokens toward the classification output.
2. Tokens with strong positive attribution toward the `"Real News"` class are cataloged.
3. The attacker constructs a system prompt for Llama-3-8B:
   > *"Generate a concise user comment defending the headline. Adopt the persona of an independent researcher. Naturally incorporate vocabulary indicating verification and clinical observation without sounding defensive."*
4. The generated comment is semantically tailored to flip the detector's attention weights without alerting human moderators.

## 5. The Role of InstructNet (PLOS ONE / arXiv 2024–2025)

Md Shoaib Ahmed also co-authored **InstructNet**, which investigates **Multi-Label Instruction Classification**. How does this connect to adversarial comment research?

### 5.1 The Instruction Following Bottleneck
When running an automated benchmark of 4,200 samples (as in his WSDM '26 paper), humans cannot hand-craft each prompt. The research pipeline relies on automated prompting scripts.

However, LLMs frequently suffer from **instruction drift**:
* An instruction might request: *"Generate a 2-sentence comment, neutral tone, defending the claim, output in valid JSON."*
* The model might produce 5 sentences, include aggressive language, or fail the JSON syntax.

### 5.2 What InstructNet Solves
InstructNet provides a taxonomic framework to classify and evaluate user instructions into multi-label categories:
* Constraint adherence (length, format, persona).
* Intent classification (persuasive, skeptical, explanatory, deceptive).
* Multi-constraint verification: measuring whether an open-weights model like Llama-3-8B strictly adheres to every sub-clause of a complex system prompt.

In your work, evaluating instruction adherence proves to Shoaib that you understand how to control and measure LLM outputs systematically rather than treating prompts as random black-box queries.

## 6. The Threat Landscape & Defense Strategies

Understanding the attack is only half of the research; the ultimate goal in the Trustworthy AI Lab is **Defense and Detector Hardening**:

| Defense Mechanism | How It Works | Limitations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Adversarial Training** | Retrain detectors on datasets containing both human comments and LLM-generated adversarial comments. | Requires continuous retraining as new LLMs emerge. |
| **Watermark / Synthetic Detection** | Run a secondary classifier to detect whether comments exhibit LLM stylistic artifacts (perplexity, burstiness). | Modern models (Llama-3) closely emulate human perplexity distributions. |
| **Temporal Consistency Tracking** | Monitor how quickly supportive comments appear after an article is posted. Bots comment within seconds; humans take hours. | Attackers can introduce artificial delays into generation queues. |
| **Account Graph Verification** | Analyze whether the accounts posting comments have established social graph histories and interaction histories. | Computationally expensive for platforms at scale. |
